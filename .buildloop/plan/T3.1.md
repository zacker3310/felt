# Plan: T3.1 POV stroke challenge

**id:** T3.1   **intent:** .buildloop/intent/T3.1.md   **spec:** to write first (step 1)
**status:** planned, not started. Build after bumper pool, practice and trick shots land.

## Shape

Three layers, built in this order, each shippable and tested on its own:

1. **Stroke model** (sim region, DOM-free, deterministic): turns a recorded stroke gesture into a
   shot `{ angle, power, spin }`. Pure function of the gesture samples, so it tests in Node.
2. **POV renderer** (new section 4h, WebGL, hand-rolled): draws the same game state from a camera
   down on the cue. Read-only over `game`/`tab`, like the 2D renderer. Falls back to the 2D view
   without WebGL.
3. **Challenge mode** (section 5/6): the mode, its shots, scoring, menu entry, progress.

The flat physics, `judge`, the CPU and every existing test are untouched: POV only changes how a
shot is chosen and how it is drawn. The live roll still equals `simulateShot` byte for byte.

## Steps

0. **Read puttX's stroke.** Its gesture capture (back-swing, forward stroke, release), how path
   deviation and tempo become start-line error and pace, its tuning constants and its tests.
   Record in the spec what carries over and what changes for a cue.
1. **Spec** `.buildloop/spec/T3.1.md`: the stroke model contract, camera, controls, challenge
   rules, acceptance criteria (below), and the decisions left open in the intent.
2. **Stroke model** in the sim region:
   - `strokeShot(samples, aim)`: samples are `{ t, x, y }` in a stroke-local frame (the line of
     the cue on screen), from first touch to release. Outputs `{ angle, power, spin, quality }`.
   - Speed: from the forward stroke's speed at the contact point (not the back-swing length), on
     the existing `speedOf`/`powerFor` scale.
   - Direction error: the stroke's lateral path and its angle at contact (a stroke that cuts
     across the line pushes the cue ball off it; puttX's left/right miss), scaled so a clean
     stroke is within about 0.1 deg and a sloppy one 1-2 deg.
   - Unintended english: a crooked stroke also hits the cue ball off-centre sideways (squirt and
     side spin come from the existing physics through `spin.x`).
   - Tempo: a jerky stroke (acceleration spikes, decelerating into the ball) adds speed error;
     a smooth accelerating stroke through the ball is rewarded.
   - Determinism: same samples, same shot. No clocks or randomness inside the function.
   - Tests (physics-check or a new `stroke-check.cjs`): a straight smooth stroke gives the aim
     angle and the intended power; a stroke curving right misses right and adds right english;
     tempo errors change power within stated bounds; fuzzed gestures never NaN or exceed bounds.
3. **Stroke input**: in POV the bottom of the screen is the stroke zone: pull back, push through,
   release. Visual feedback: the cue moves 1:1 with the finger, a faint "straight line" guide
   that the stroke should follow, and after the shot a stroke replay trace (path, tempo, where it
   went wrong). Desktop: mouse drag the same way. The power bar is not used in POV.
4. **POV renderer** (`index.html` section 4h, WebGL):
   - Camera: behind and above the cue ball along the aim, eye height about 12-25 cm above the
     cloth, 40-60 cm back; field of view about 50 deg; smooth swoop from the top view and back.
   - Scene: the cloth as a plane textured with the existing `tableImg` art (already drawn from the
     geometry globals, so pockets, generous style and cloths match), rails and cushions extruded
     from `CUSHION_POLYS` with simple lighting, pockets as dark wells, balls as shaded spheres
     (stripes, numbers, the game-ball halo) using the 2D renderer's colours, soft contact
     shadows, the cue as a tapered cylinder with tip and ferrule.
   - Aiming in POV: drag left/right turns the aim (fine), the fine wheel still works, no aim
     guide (that is the challenge), optional "ghost" for the easiest tier.
   - After the stroke: the camera stays for a beat, then rises to the top view to watch the roll
     (setting: stay or rise).
   - Performance: 60 fps on an iPhone at dpr 3 with 16 balls; budget per frame measured in a
     render test with a software fallback check.
5. **Challenge mode**:
   - A ladder of shots (straight-in short, straight-in long, cuts at 15/30/45 deg, a stop shot,
     a draw shot, a bank), each scored on the stroke (`quality`), pot or miss, and for position
     shots where the cue ball ends. Three lives or a fixed number of attempts; a best score saved.
   - Difficulty tiers by pocket style (generous first) and guide (a ghost-ball hint on tier 1).
   - Menu: "POV challenge" next to Practice and Trick shots; pause menu shows score and attempts.
6. **Optional, after the challenge**: POV as an aiming view in normal games (toggle), using the
   power bar instead of the stroke, if Zac wants it (open question in the intent).

## Acceptance (to be finalised in the spec)

- A clean stroke pots a straight-in ball every time; a stroke curving 1 cm sideways over its last
  5 cm misses a 1 m straight-in shot on the side it curved (stroke-check).
- Same samples, same result; the live roll equals `simulateShot` (play-check).
- POV draws the same layout as the top view: projecting each ball's 3D position back to the
  table matches `game.balls` (render-check).
- 60 fps budget at dpr 3 in the render test (headless proxy, as for the loupe).
- Falls back to the top view with no WebGL, no console errors (boot-check).
- The challenge saves progress; every challenge shot is solvable with a clean stroke (solver test
  like trick shots).

## Risks

- **Stroke feel on glass.** A phone screen has no resistance; puttX's tuning was for a putt.
  Signal: playtest shows strokes feel random. Mitigation: tune error scaling and smoothing with
  recorded real strokes from Zac's phone before tuning by eye.
- **WebGL on iOS**: context loss when the app is backgrounded. Signal: black view after resume.
  Mitigation: handle `webglcontextlost`/`restored`, rebuild textures from `tableImg`.
- **Single-file size**: a WebGL renderer adds a large block to `index.html`. Keep it in its own
  section with its own region markers; no libraries.
- **Two renderers drifting**: POV must read the same geometry globals and colours as the 2D
  renderer. Signal: a render-check that projects POV ball positions back to the table fails.
