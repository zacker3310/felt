How to play

Aim
Point at the cloth, or click a spot; drag on the table to swing the cue round the cue ball. ← → fine-tune (Shift coarse, Alt fine), and so does the ridged wheel beside the power bar (drag it or scroll on it). A fine-tuned aim holds still until the mouse moves away or clicks, and the aim never follows the pointer off the cloth. Auto aim starts each turn pointed at the ball to play next and marks a pocket for it: the cut is up to you.
Shoot
Pull along the power bar (down, or right when it lies flat). Past a short dead zone the cue draws back: let go to shoot, the further the harder. To cancel, come back past the start or slide off the bar. Nothing else shoots: not the table, not a key.
English
Move the blue tip on the cue ball: high for follow, low for draw, the sides for english. Double click it or press R to centre.
In hand
The cue ball starts on a legal spot; drag it to move it, as often as you like. Letting go never locks it: only the shot does. Let go somewhere illegal and it goes back to the last good spot. Or press M, move it with the arrows (Shift for bigger steps) and press M again to aim.
Pause
The pause button or Esc. The game waits. Esc also closes a sheet like this one.
Aim
Tap where the cue ball should go, or drag anywhere on the table to swing the cue: it follows your finger round the cue ball. For the last hair, roll the ridged wheel beside the power bar. With auto aim on, each turn starts pointed at the ball to play next, with a pocket marked for it: the cut is up to you. The table only aims: it never shoots.
Shoot
Pull along the power bar. Past a short dead zone the cue draws back on the table: let go to shoot, the further the harder. To cancel, slide back to the start or off the bar.
English
Move the blue tip on the cue ball: high for follow, low for draw, the sides for english. Double tap it to centre.
In hand
The cue ball starts on a legal spot. Drag it (start on or next to it) to move it, as often as you like: letting go never locks it, only the shot does. Let go somewhere illegal and it goes back to the last good spot. A drag anywhere else still aims.
Pause
The pause button. The game waits.
Break
Ball in hand behind the head string. An 8 potted on the break is spotted and the breaker carries on.
Groups
The table is open until the first legal pot after the break: that player takes that group, solids (1 to 7) or stripes (9 to 15), and the other player the rest.
Shot
Hit a ball of your group first (the 8 once your group is gone), then pot a ball or send any ball to a rail. Any pocket counts. Pot one of yours and you go again.
Fouls
The wrong ball first, no rail after contact, or the cue ball down: your opponent gets ball in hand anywhere.
Win
Clear your group, then pot the 8. Pot the 8 before your group is clear, or scratch while potting it, and you lose.
Rack
Nine balls in a diamond: the 1 at the front, the 9 in the middle. Break with ball in hand behind the head string.
Shot
Always hit the lowest ball on the table first, then pot a ball or hit a rail. Whatever you pot counts, so combinations and caroms are fine.
Fouls
Ball in hand anywhere for your opponent. Three fouls in a row lose the game; the chip and the pause menu warn on two.
Win
Pot the 9 on a legal shot, whenever it comes: early in a combination or last.
Bumper
Red against white, five balls each, on a small table with two cups and twelve rubber posts. You strike your own balls (no cue ball) at the cup at the far end; the marked ball (the one with the spot) must go down before any other of yours. Tap one of your balls (or press B) to choose the one to strike; auto aim picks one and a line.
Opening
Red shoots the marked ball, then white. Whoever potted theirs shoots next (red if both); if neither did, the one nearer the far cup. A foul on white's opening gives red the table.
Turn
Pot one of yours in the far cup and you go again; otherwise the turn passes.
Fouls
Striking a ball not your own; a ball of yours in your own cup; an unmarked ball of yours before your marked one; potting your opponent's ball (it counts for them); a ball off the table. Your balls that dropped on a foul come back to your marked spot, and your opponent drops one of theirs in: the marked ball if it is still up, else the one furthest from their cup.
Win
The first with all five down. Choose the classic octagon or the rectangle when you start a bumper game.
Snooker
Fifteen reds and six colours. Pot a red (1 point), then a colour: the first colour the cue ball hits is the one you are on. Then a red again, and so on. Colours go back on their spots while reds remain. With the reds gone, pot the colours in order: yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7.
Break
Ball in hand in the D, the half circle on the baulk line: drag the cue ball anywhere inside it. Hit a red first. The usual break clips the end red of the back row and brings the cue ball back to baulk.
Fouls
Missing every ball, hitting or potting a ball you are not on, or the cue ball going in (an in-off). Your opponent scores the value of the ball on or the highest ball involved: at least 4, at most 7. After an in-off they play from the D, otherwise from where the cue ball stopped.
Frame
Most points wins. With only the black left, the first score or foul ends the frame; if that leaves the scores level, the black is re-spotted and the other player plays it from the D. Concede a frame from the pause menu. Choose one frame or the best of three, and the compact 10 ft or the full 12 ft table, when you start.
Score
The chips show the scores; the bar on the top rail shows the break, the ball on and the points left, and says when snookers are needed. Not in felt yet: the free ball and the miss rule.
Practice
No opponent and no fouls, always your shot. Undo takes back the last shot; the tools button holds ball in hand, reset rack and, on a free table, adding and removing balls.
Tricks
One layout, one goal. Three stars for a make on the first try, two within three tries, one for any make. Show me plays one way to make it.
Daily
One trick shot a day, the same for everyone that day. Your first try counts: play on consecutive days and your streak grows, miss a day and it starts again. Retries are practice.
